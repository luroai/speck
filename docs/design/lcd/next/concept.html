Fidelity

More dots, same screen

The first sketch drew the ring 16 dots across and moved at two frames a second. This one fits two and a half times as many dots across the same width, so curves come out round and small parts like the ears and lids have room to act.

Transitions

From one state to the next

Idle to celebrating, frame by frame. Every change of state takes 0.7 seconds, so the character never jumps from one drawing to another.

The pose blends

Where it looks, how it leans, its arms and the ring’s opening all travel between the two states. Arms grow out of the shoulder, and fold back into it.

It blinks and hops

A small hop through the change and a blink at its midpoint, so the switch reads as a decision. The blink also hides the moment the eye changes shape.

The dots stay crisp

Each dot switches on at once and clears within a frame, so even the fast parts stay sharp. Props dissolve in and out through a 4 × 4 dither.

Logos

One mark, every size

The mark is one 16 × 16 bitmap, drawn by hand so it’s sharp in a browser tab. At larger sizes it sits on the LCD, one pixel of the bitmap to one dot. Everywhere it appears has a light and a dark version.

One system

The same screen everywhere

The landing page, the panel and anything built with speck after them use one screen, one character and one set of rules.

The screen

Reflective by day, backlit by night

In the light look it’s a reflective LCD, its dots casting a faint shadow on the glass behind. In the dark look it turns negative, pale dots on a dark screen, like a backlit car stereo.

The dot

One unit of speck, one dot

Landing160 × 96 dots, a 4 px dot, 1 dot per unit
Badge28 × 28 dots in 56 px, 0.32 dots per unit
Auto-build14 × 14 dots in 28 px
Mark16 × 16 dots, still

The drawing is defined once, in the panel’s units, and drawn at whatever size the screen has.

The line

One weight, snapped to the grid

Every line is two dots wide on the big screens and one in the panel, and it keeps that weight as it moves because strokes snap to the dots. The ring is always open at the top left. It closes only to celebrate.

Colour

Ink, and one vermilion speck

The screen has one colour besides its ink: the speck’s vermilion eye. Status colour stays off the screen and goes on its frame. A badge waiting for you gets a purple frame, and one that needs you gets a crimson one, the panel’s own colours for those states.

Emphasis

Solid, blinking, then inverted

A screen this small can’t rely on colour to show state. Code the builder looks at goes solid, a line under discussion blinks, and anything checked or approved turns inverted. This replaces the panel’s green.

Motion

Blend, hop, blink

States blend over 0.7 s with a hop and a blink, and props dissolve through a dither. Dots switch on at once and clear within a frame, so nothing smears. With reduced motion on, each state is a single still frame.

What the panel would change

  1. The badge’s canvas drawing is replaced by this renderer. The seven poses, their timings, their labels and the 56 px tile stay as they are.
  2. A ticket moving from one stage to the next blends its badge into the new pose instead of cutting to it.
  3. Four new colour tokens, for the screen, its unlit dots, its ink and the speck, in both looks.
  4. Checked code turns inverted instead of green. In review, the floating characters become one small page of code: a line blinks while it’s discussed, and the page inverts when it’s approved. The shipping boat loses its tick.
  5. The Auto-build button’s hopping builder uses the same screen, and the header, the favicon and the home-screen icons take the new mark.
